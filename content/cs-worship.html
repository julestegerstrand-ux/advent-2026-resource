Worship on the First Sunday after Christmas Day

The primary goal of worship on this first Sunday after Christmas Day is to be sure that we are celebrating Christmas! All Advent we have been looking forward to it, and now we enter the celebration. Christmas lasts 12 days! (There's a song because it lasts 12 days, not the other way around!). Sing Christmas carols and songs of celebration! Don't allow the culture, which has already moved on to Valentine's Day, to dictate how we celebrate Christmas. We tell time differently. Help your people with this concept. Keep the decorations up, the Christ candle lit, and the songs of Christmas alive!

Worship Themes to Consider

  • Christmas celebration - Christmas has just begun!
  • The Incarnation: This is the core of the celebration—the idea that God became human in the person of Jesus. This theme can be explored through scripture and sermon, emphasizing the invisible becoming visible and the absolute becoming particular.
  • Hope, Peace, and Salvation: The birth of Jesus is Good News that brings hope, peace, and salvation to the world. Worship can focus on these gifts and how they can fuel optimism and guide our lives.
  • God's Love: The Christmas story is a reminder of God's continual love for humanity. Themes can revolve around the love that came down and the gift that changed the world.

Christmas Sunday Call to Worship

Leader: In the quiet of the night, a message of great joy is given to us.

Congregation: Fear not, for behold, we bring you good news of great joy for all people.

Leader: God's promise, revealed in the birth of his Son, fills us with wonder.

Congregation: We marvel at the purest and most precious gift, the gift of your Son.

Leader: We are called to be instruments of that good news, to be a community of faith.

Congregation: Our joy comes from community, from seeing God's kingdom come on earth as it is in heaven.

Leader: For in the birth of Jesus, God is with us, Emmanuel.

Congregation: Emmanuel, God-with-us, your light lifts up our hearts and voices.

Leader: Let us live out this good news, to embody the word of your love.

Congregation: May we celebrate the joy of Christ's coming, and may the love of Jesus Christ the Son shine forth from within us, both now and forevermore.

We can also use the Psalms reading as a call to worship, or elsewhere in the worship flow. Each week, we have the Psalm in the form of a responsive reading, which is a great option. It adds additional Scripture in our worship and also gives congregational participation in a way beyond singing.
