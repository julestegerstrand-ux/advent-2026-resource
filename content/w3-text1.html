Psalm 126 is a Psalm of joy. It is part of the collection of Psalms we know as the Songs of Ascent, traveling psalms to be used by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem. The tone of the Psalm moves from astonished, communal joy to an earnest prayer for ongoing restoration. The Psalm connects to the season of Advent primarily through the theme of joy. It is important to note both the communal reality of joy in the midst of the journey, as well as the reminder that sorrow and joy are interwoven in life. There is hope, however, in the promise that the long night of sorrow will not endure forever. "They that sow in tears will reap in joy!" Advent points ahead to the reason to proclaim, "the Lord has done great tings for us!" I appreciate what author, Paul Anthony Carr wrote about this psalm:

"Psalm 126 is a psalm of trouble, but it's a psalm of trouble with a twist. It's a psalm that offers you hope when you are in the midst of trouble and tears. It tells us that times of trouble and sorrow do not last. God will turn your sorrow to joy and your tears to laughter. Weeping may endure for the night, but joy comes in the morning. And one day we will leave all the sorrows of this world behind for the eternal joys that await us in heaven." (from the sermon, "Psalm 126: God's Promise of Enduring Love")

Major Themes

  • Astonished Joy (Verses 1–3): The people describe their release from exile as feeling "like those who dream", filled with laughter and singing so striking that even neighboring nations acknowledged God's work.
  • Prayer for Renewal (Verse 4): The community asks God to fully restore their fortunes, comparing the request to seasonal desert streams (Negev) flowing back to life with water.
  • Sowing and Reaping (Verses 5–6): A profound promise that hardship and grief ("sowing in tears") will ultimately culminate in a harvest of gladness ("reaping in joy")

Outline of Psalm 126 from The Outline Bible

The psalmist remembers God's great deliverance and prays it will continue until completion. He describes the nation Judah's emotion upon being released from a terrible captivity.

1. THE REALITY (126:1: At first the freed captives have difficulty comprehending the truth of this wonderful event.

A. Among the people (126:2-3): They are filled with laughter and testify to God's faithfulness.
