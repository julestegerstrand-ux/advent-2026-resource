Including lament in a Christmas Eve service involves acknowledging sorrow and brokenness while holding onto resolute hope for God's kingdom to come. can be done through intercessory prayers that name contemporary issues like injustice or division, selecting hymns that combine sorrow with expectation (like "O Come, O Come, Immanuel"), and incorporating readings or personal testimonies that speak to suffering and the yearning for God's intervention. The goal is to provide an honest, faith-filled response to pain and an affirmation of belief in God's promises, ultimately leading to recommitment and a deeper longing for the fulfilled reign of Christ.

Why Include Lament?

  • Honesty about the world:
    Acknowledging the brokenness, chaos, and suffering in the world acknowledges that the birth of Jesus did not immediately eliminate all pain and injustice,
  • Biblical lament is a Godly response to suffering and injustice, which ultimately seeks to recommit one's hope in God's promises, according to LibGuides.
  • A more complete expression of Advent:
    It allows for a more holistic expression of the Advent season, which is not only about the joyous anticipation of Jesus' birth but also about the deep longing for a future where God's kingdom has fully come.
  • Deepens faith:
    By voicing our pain and sorrow, it can strengthen faith and trust in God and his promises for the future, according to The Village Church.

How to Include Lament in a Christmas Eve Service

  • Prayers of Lament:
    Use specific prayers that name current issues and lament the presence of sin, suffering, and division in the community and world.
  • Hymns and Songs:
    Incorporate hymns that blend sorrow with hope, such as "O Come, O Come, Immanuel," which is "O come, O King of Nations" and "O Come, O Bright and Morning Star," which expresses hope in the midst of darkness.
  • Scripture Readings:
    Read passages from scripture that address lament, such as Old Testament prophetic books or even a modern translation by someone like Rev. Dr. Wilda Gafney.
  • Personal Testimonies:
    Invite members of the congregation to share brief, personal stories of suffering or struggle, followed by a recommitment to hope in Christ.
  • Liturgical Elements:
    Design elements of the service to be contemplative rather than solely joyous, such as a time for reflective silence or a more somber reading of the Christmas narrative before the joyful parts of the service.
  • Theme of Hope:
    Ensure the lament is balanced with a message of resolute hope in God's future kingdom, leading to a joyful conclusion.
