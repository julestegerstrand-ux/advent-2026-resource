Embracing Love While We Wait

The theme for the third week of Advent is love. Love is an overused word in the English language and can mean very different things in very different contexts. I love my wife, but I also love burritos. Certainly, the word carries different meanings depending on how we use it. We use love to describe our affections, our preferences, our relationships, and sometimes even our passing interests. But what does it mean to embody and embrace love in response to God's love?

Advent invites us to remember that love is not sentimentality but a way of being in the world. The love we anticipate in the coming of Christ is a love that draws near to the brokenhearted, embraces those pushed to the margins, and refuses to accept that the suffering of others is the way things must be. Embracing love calls us beyond feelings and into action.

In Luke 1:26–38, Mary receives the unexpected news that she will bear Jesus, the embodiment of God's love in the world. Though she does not fully understand what lies ahead, Mary responds with courage and openness to God's invitation. Her story reminds us that embracing love sometimes means saying yes to God even when the path forward is uncertain, trusting that God's love will hold us as we participate in God's work of redemption.

In 2 Samuel, 7:1-11, 16, God reminds David that though he wants to build a house for God, that God is actually preparing a place for David and his people. Christians affirm that promise points beyond David to Jesus, the one through whom God's love takes flesh and dwells among us. God does not wait for us to love God. God loves us first. Every act of love that we embody in the world is ultimately in response to God's love directed toward us.

Paul's closing words in Romans 16:25-27 remind us that the mystery of God's love, revealed through Jesus Christ, is made known to all nations that is all people! God's love is not limited to one people, one community, or one way of life. It is a love that reaches outward, inviting the whole world into the life God desires for us. Author Cole Arthur Riley affirms this in reference to the doctrine of the Trinity in her book This Here Flesh: Spirituality, Liberation, and the Stories That Make Us. "People talk about God as three distinct persons in one. If this is true, it means the whole cosmos is predicated on a diverse and holy community. And if we bear the image of God, that means we bear the image of a multitude. And that to bear the image of God in its fullness, we need each other." Riley's words remind us that love is not about making everyone the same but about learning to live together in the fullness of our differences. As we wait for Christ, we embrace love by building communities where our differences are not simply tolerated but celebrated as essential to reflecting the fullness of God's image.

As we wait in Advent, we are called to participate in making that love known, not simply through what we believe, but through how we treat our neighbors, how we respond to violence, and how we create communities where others experience belonging. We wait for the fullness of God's love to be revealed, even as we embody that love in the world today. Even when the world gives us reasons to despair, Advent reminds us that love is still at work, and we are called to bear witness to it. Amen.
