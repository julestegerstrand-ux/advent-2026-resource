Psalm 80 is a communal lament, attributed to Asaph. Interestingly, this is a psalm that includes the musical reference: For the director of music. To the tune of “The Lilies of the Covenant.” This psalm cries out for the restoration of Israel, likely following the Assyrian destruction of the northern kingdom in 722 BC. It calls on God as Israel's Shepherd and Gardener, pleading for Him to awaken His strength, shine His face upon His people, and deliver them from their enemies, who are depicted as wild animals destroying a flourishing vine. The psalm uses a refrain, "Restore us, O God; make your face shine upon us, that we may be saved," to emphasize the people's trust in God's favor and restoration.

Thinking of this Psalm in the context of Advent has several elements. At a fundamental level, it is the cry, "Come and save us!" This longing for God's coming is at the core of Advent. The psalm also centers on the image of "the Shepherd of Israel, you who lead Joseph like a flock." Because this psalm is a lament, it also invites opportunities to acknowledge sorrow in the midst of the hope of Advent, remembering that both include longing.

We also find here the invocation of "the son of man you have raised up for yourself," and while the phrase has meaning in its original context, we remember that the term is one that Jesus used more than any other to refer to himself.

The psalm gives us an Advent prayer: "Restore us, Lord God Almighty; make your face shine on us, that we may be saved."

(It is interesting to note that this same Psalm text is used here in Year B for Week One.) and again in Year A for Week Four.

Responsive Reading of Psalm 80

Outline of Psalm 80 from The Outline Bible

Asaph cries out to God because his people are scorned by their enemies. He recalls God's past goodness to them and asks for favor once again. Asaph uses three images to describe God's people and their relationship to him.

I. THE SHEEP AND THE SHEPHERD (80:1-7): Asaph calls God the Shepherd of Israel.

A. Listen to us (80:1-2): Asaph wants God, who is "enthroned above the cherubim," to respond to his people's cries and rescue

  • Do not be angry with us (80:4-6): God has rejected them and made them the scorn of nations.
  • Redeem your people (80:3, 7): Asaph asks God to cause his people to turn to him again so they can be saved.

II. THE VINE AND THE VINEYARD OWNER (80:8-16)

A. The past vine (80:8-11)

  1. It is brought out of Egypt as a tender vine (80:8).
  2. It is brought into Canaan, where it flourishes (80:8-11).
  • The present vine (80:12-13, 16): It is punished for its fruitless-ness.
  • The future vine (80:14-15): The psalmist begs God to return and care once again for his vine.

III. THE SINNERS AND THE SAVIOR (80:17-19)

  • The person (80:17): "Strengthen the man you love, the son of your choice" is most likely a reference to the Messiah.
  • The plea (80:18-19): "Make your face shine down upon us" is a plea for God to turn toward them and save them.
