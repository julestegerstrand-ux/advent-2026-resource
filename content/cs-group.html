Small Group Handout

Here are some additional small group ideas:

1. What would you take?
This activity encourages empathy and reflection on what is truly important when forced to leave everything behind.

  • Prompt: Imagine you are Joseph and Mary, with a newborn baby. An angel warns you to flee with only what you can carry. You have to leave your home, your community, and everything you know.
  • Activity: Set a timer for 5 minutes. Have each group member write down or gather a few items they would grab in that short amount of time.
  • Discussion: Share the choices people made. Discuss what was hard to leave behind, and what it might feel like to be a refugee in a foreign land. How does this shed light on the plight of refugees today and on God's call for us to care for them?

2. The dark side of Advent
The story of Herod's massacre is a reminder that Advent is not always a joyous, sentimental time. It's a period of "holy tension" between the promised hope and the brokeness of the world.

  • Prompt: Matthew 2:16–18 describes Herod's horrific actions. Why do you think Matthew included this dark and violent part of the story, especially right after the joyful visit of the Magi?
  • Discussion:
    • Discuss the reality of suffering, evil, and doubt in our world, even during Christmas.
    • How does the story challenge our typical expectations of the Advent season?
    • Where do you see signs of "darkness" in the world today that make you long for Christ's return?

3. Trusting God in uncertainty
Joseph had to trust God's direction repeatedly, even when it meant uprooting his family and moving to an unknown place.

  • Prompt: In the passage, Joseph is warned in a dream to go to Egypt, and then later in a dream is told to return to Israel, eventually settling in Nazareth because of a warning about Archelaus.
  • Activity: Have members silently reflect on a time when they had to make a major life decision based on a quiet prompting or "leap of faith," without a clear or certain outcome.
  • Discussion:
    • How did you experience God's guidance during that time?
    • What made trusting God difficult or frightening?
    • How does Joseph's consistent obedience, even in confusing circumstances, encourage you in your own life?.

4. The "hope box"
This activity helps group members visualize giving their anxieties and hopes to God.

  • Activity: Provide each member with a small box or container (e.g., a decorated matchbox or small tin). Instruct them to write down specific fears, anxieties, or uncertainties they are facing this season on small pieces of paper. On the other side of the paper, they write a corresponding hope or prayer for God's provision.
  • Instructions: Put the papers inside the box and decorate it with a heart or a caption like "hidden in his heart, covered by God's love."
  • Discussion: After the activity, briefly discuss the experience. Keep the boxes as a tangible reminder to trust in God's protection

5. Advent tags
This activity focuses on personal growth during Advent.

  • Activity: Give each person a blank tag (like a luggage tag) and string. On one side of the tag, have them write something they want to "peel away" during Advent (e.g., busyness, impatience, materialism). On the other side, they write a trait they want to grow (e.g., hope, peace, courage).
  • Instructions: Tie the tag to a small bare branch or an indoor plant. As the plant grows or gets decorated, it can serve as a reminder of new growth.
  • Discussion: Share and pray over the tags. How does Matthew's account of suffering and protection inspire a different kind of "hope" and "courage"?.
